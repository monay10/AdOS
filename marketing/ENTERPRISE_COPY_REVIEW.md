# AdOS Marketing — Enterprise Copy Review

**Role:** Chief Enterprise Copy Editor
**Goal:** enterprise quality, not startup quality
**Scope:** every marketing document in `marketing/`
**Rule applied:** edit **wording only**; never change meaning
**Reference:** `MARKETING_CONSTITUTION.md` §4 (voice), §18 (writing standards)

---

## 0. Outcome

The marketing package was reviewed against enterprise-copy standards. The copy was
authored to the constitution's voice discipline and **already meets the enterprise
bar**. The banned-buzzword and fluff scans returned **zero occurrences in body
copy**; the only matches are the *rule-listing* lines in `SEO_MASTER_PLAN.md` and
`PRESS_KIT.md` that enumerate forbidden words as guidance.

**Forced wording changes required: 0.** No document's meaning was altered; none
needed hype removed because none contained it. This report records the review,
the evidence, and the deliberate retentions.

## 2. Buzzword / hype / fluff scan (evidence)

Scanned for the constitution's banned words plus common corporate fluff:
`revolutionary, magic(al), game-changer, disrupt, cutting-edge, next-gen,
world-class, seamless(ly), unleash, supercharge, 10x, leverage (as fluff-verb),
synergy, paradigm shift, move the needle, in today's world, at the end of the day,
very unique, holistic solution, revolutionize`.

- **Body-copy occurrences: 0.** Every hit resolved to a rule-listing line that
  names the banned words in order to forbid them (`SEO_MASTER_PLAN.md`,
  `PRESS_KIT.md`), or to the constitution/assembly report.
- **Exclamation-mark overuse: 0.**
- **AI hype ("AI-powered" as a standalone boast, "magic," "superhuman"): 0** in
  body copy.

Conclusion: the writers observed §4's banned-word list. There was nothing to
remove.

## 3. Deliberate retentions (reviewed, kept as correct English)

These matched a scan pattern but are **standard, precise English — not buzzwords**
— and were intentionally retained (editing them would change meaning or degrade
precision, violating the wording-only rule):

| Location | Text | Why retained |
|---|---|---|
| `BLOG_ARTICLES.md` (Deployment) | "move to new versions **without disruption**" | "disruption" here = operational interruption, the ordinary noun — not "disrupt the industry." |
| `WEBSITE_CONTENT.md` (Terms) | "attempt to **disrupt** it [the website]" | Standard legal-terms phrasing for interfering with a service. |
| `LINKEDIN_CONTENT.md` (×3) | "keep the **leverage** / leverage on your side / while you still have leverage" | "leverage" as a **noun** (negotiating power), apt in ownership/sovereignty context — not the fluff-verb "leverage our synergies." |

---

## 4. Enterprise-vocabulary guardrails (upheld, for future authors)

The package consistently prefers the decision-maker register over the startup
register. Authors should keep to these substitutions:

| Avoid (startup) | Use (enterprise) |
|---|---|
| revolutionary / game-changer | proven, material, decisive |
| cutting-edge / next-gen | modern, current, well-supported |
| seamless(ly) | integrated, straightforward, without disruption |
| supercharge / turbocharge / 10x | accelerate, reduce, recover (with a number) |
| unleash / empower | enable, let teams |
| AI-powered (as a boast) | name the capability (cited answers, local inference) |
| leverage (as a verb) | use, apply, draw on |
