## 0. Purpose and scope

This is the single SEO plan of record for AdOS. It governs keyword strategy,
site structure, on-page and technical SEO, structured data, performance, and the
12-month roadmap — for **both** the English and Turkish sites, treated as equal
tracks rather than a primary market with a translated afterthought.

It inherits the canonical facts verbatim: AdOS is a **sovereign, on-premise
Enterprise AI Operating System for Advertising** — a marketing-performance **Company
Brain** plus a **human-approved campaign pipeline** (brief → creative copy →
campaign draft → performance report → executive dashboard) — that runs **100% on the
customer's own infrastructure**, offline and air-gap capable, with **no cloud, no
external API, no per-token billing, no vendor lock-in**. It **drafts** campaigns; it
does not launch live ads. Every keyword, page, and claim in this plan must be
consistent with those facts. We rank by teaching the buyer to want sovereignty —
never by claiming capabilities AdOS does not have.

**Non-negotiables carried into SEO:**
- Bilingual parity: no target keyword, page, or claim exists in one language only.
- Product terms stay in English in both languages: **AdOS**, **Company Brain**. We
  never translate product names, even in TR titles.
- Honest performance framing: we may target "fast on your hardware" intents but we
  never imply millisecond frontier-cloud latency on local CPU.
- Honest scope: AdOS **drafts** campaigns for human approval; we never target
  "launch/run/optimize live ads" intents as a present capability.
- Category ownership: we are building **"Sovereign Advertising AI"** /
  **"Enterprise AI Operating System for Advertising"** as a category, not chasing
  generic "AI tool" head terms we cannot win or honestly serve.

## 3. Internal linking

Inherits the Website IA §7 hub-and-spoke model; this section makes it SEO-explicit.

**Architecture:**
- **Super-hub:** Home (`/en/`, `/tr/`) — passes authority to the three pillars.
- **Pillars:** Local AI, On-Prem & Offline, Security — mutually cross-linked.
- **Spokes:** blog posts link **up** to their pillar; pillars link **down** to
  their best spokes and **laterally** to sibling pillars.
- **Universal sink:** Demo (`/demo`) — every content page offers a demo next-step.

**Rules:**
- Every spoke links to its pillar within the first two paragraphs, with a
  keyword-descriptive anchor (never "click here", "read more").
- Every pillar links to the other two pillars and up to its parent
  (`/solutions`, `/security`).
- Product pages link to ≥1 pillar and to Demo.
- Related-content block (2–3 contextual links) at the foot of every
  non-conversion page.
- **No orphans:** every page reachable from header, footer, or a parent within 2
  clicks of Home.
- Anchor-text discipline: vary anchors around the target term's synonyms; avoid
  exact-match over-optimization.
- **Cross-language links are `hreflang` only** — the TR page links to TR pages;
  we do not mix a TR body link to an EN page (except intentional `/docs` outbound).
- Breadcrumb links (BreadcrumbList, §5/§9 of IA) reinforce hierarchy on every page
  except Home/Demo.
- Link priority follows IA `L#`: L0/L1 pages receive the most inbound internal
  links; L3 legal/blog receive fewer (footer + contextual only).

## 4. Canonical rules

- **Self-referencing canonical on every page.** Each page's `<link rel="canonical">`
  points to its own clean, absolute, locale-prefixed URL (`https://…/en/solutions/local-ai`).
- **One canonical per language.** The EN page is canonical for EN; the TR page is
  canonical for TR. Neither language canonicalizes to the other — that is what
  `hreflang` is for (§14).
- **Absolute HTTPS URLs**, lowercase, no trailing slash, matching the sitemap
  exactly.
- **Parameters:** analytics/routing params (`?track=security`, UTM) never change
  the canonical — the canonical is always the clean path. Content is never gated
  behind a required query string.
- **Pagination:** paginated blog listings self-canonicalize per page (no
  canonical-to-page-1); `rel=next/prev` deprecated by Google, so rely on clean
  crawlable links.
- **No duplicate roots:** enforce a single host (`www` vs apex decided once) and
  HTTPS-only; all variants 301 to the canonical host.
- **Trailing-slash consistency:** pick "no trailing slash" (per IA §10) and 301
  the slashed variant.
- **HTTP status hygiene:** canonical URLs must return 200; never canonicalize to a
  redirecting or noindex URL.

## 8. Robots

**Policy:** open the whole content site to crawlers; keep out only system,
duplicate, and non-content routes. Never block CSS/JS (Google needs them to render
and score Core Web Vitals). No locale auto-redirect that hides content from
crawlers (IA §8).

**Example `robots.txt`:**

```
# https://ados.example/robots.txt
User-agent: *
Allow: /

# Do not crawl analytics/routing-parameter duplicates
Disallow: /*?track=
Disallow: /*&track=

# System / non-content
Disallow: /404
Disallow: /500
Disallow: /*?*utm_

# Assets needed for rendering must remain crawlable (do NOT block CSS/JS)
Allow: /assets/

# Sitemaps (both languages in one index)
Sitemap: https://ados.example/sitemap.xml
```

**Rules:**
- `robots.txt` disallow controls *crawling*, not indexing — use `<meta name="robots"
  content="noindex">` (not robots.txt) for pages that must stay out of the index
  (e.g. thank-you pages, gated confirmations).
- Public content pages are `index,follow`. Legal pages are indexable but
  low-priority. `/demo` success/confirmation states are `noindex`.
- No `Disallow: /` ever ships to production; guard against staging robots leaking
  to prod (staging is protected by auth + `noindex`, not robots alone).

## 9. Sitemap

**Structure:** a sitemap **index** referencing per-type, per-language sitemaps.
Every URL entry carries `hreflang` alternates so Google sees the EN/TR pairing.

```
/sitemap.xml                      (index)
 ├── /sitemap-pages-en.xml         (EN core pages)
 ├── /sitemap-pages-tr.xml         (TR core pages)
 ├── /sitemap-blog-en.xml          (EN blog)
 ├── /sitemap-blog-tr.xml          (TR blog)
 └── /sitemap-images.xml           (key imagery, optional)
```

**Example entry with hreflang (in `sitemap-pages-en.xml`):**

```xml
<url>
  <loc>https://ados.example/en/solutions/local-ai</loc>
  <lastmod>2026-01-15</lastmod>
  <changefreq>monthly</changefreq>
  <priority>0.9</priority>
  <xhtml:link rel="alternate" hreflang="en" href="https://ados.example/en/solutions/local-ai"/>
  <xhtml:link rel="alternate" hreflang="tr" href="https://ados.example/tr/solutions/local-ai"/>
  <xhtml:link rel="alternate" hreflang="x-default" href="https://ados.example/en/solutions/local-ai"/>
</url>
```

**Rules:**
- Only canonical, 200-status, indexable URLs appear in the sitemap — no redirects,
  no noindex, no parameters.
- `priority` mirrors IA link priority (L0/L1 → 0.8–1.0; L2 → 0.5–0.7; L3 → 0.3).
- `lastmod` is truthful (updated only when content materially changes).
- Both `sitemap.xml` and `robots.txt` exist as system routes (IA §1).
- Submitted to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools for **both** language
  properties.

## 10. Page speed strategy

Speed is a ranking and conversion lever and a Constitution health metric (Core Web
Vitals pass rate, §21). The site is a Vite build (static/SSG-friendly), which we
exploit.

**Tactics:**
- **Ship static/prerendered HTML** for every content page (SSG or prerender) so
  first paint doesn't wait on JS.
- **Critical CSS inlined**; defer non-critical CSS; no render-blocking third-party
  CSS.
- **JS budget:** minimal hydration; code-split by route; defer/async all
  non-critical scripts; no heavy client frameworks on content pages beyond what's
  needed.
- **Fonts:** self-hosted, `font-display: swap`, preload the one primary sans,
  subset to Latin + Turkish glyphs ( İ, ı, ş, ğ, ç, ö, ü) to keep files small.
- **Third parties:** consent-gated analytics/heatmaps load *after* interaction /
  consent (IA §12–13), never blocking first paint.
- **Caching:** long-lived immutable cache headers on hashed assets; CDN edge
  caching for the public site (public marketing content only — this is the
  website, not the on-prem product).
- **Preconnect/preload** only same-origin critical resources; avoid external hosts.
- **HTTP/2+**, Brotli/gzip compression, no unused CSS/JS shipped.

**Budgets (per page, content pages):** HTML < 30 KB gz · CSS < 60 KB · JS < 120 KB
gz · total transferred (initial) < 500 KB · TTFB < 0.6 s.

## 11. Core Web Vitals

Targets are the "good" thresholds at the 75th percentile (field data), which we
hold ourselves above.

| Metric | Target (p75) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **LCP** (Largest Contentful Paint) | ≤ 2.0 s (good ≤ 2.5 s) | Hero text/image; preload the LCP image, no lazy-load on it |
| **INP** (Interaction to Next Paint) | ≤ 150 ms (good ≤ 200 ms) | Keep main-thread light; debounce menu/theme toggles |
| **CLS** (Cumulative Layout Shift) | ≤ 0.05 (good ≤ 0.1) | Reserve space for images/embeds; no injected banners that shift layout |
| TTFB | ≤ 0.6 s | Static/prerendered + CDN |
| FCP | ≤ 1.2 s | Inline critical CSS |

**How we hit them:**
- LCP: prerender, inline critical CSS, `fetchpriority="high"` + `preload` on the
  hero image, self-hosted fonts with `swap`.
- INP: minimal JS, event delegation, avoid long tasks; theme/lang toggles must not
  cause layout thrash.
- CLS: explicit `width`/`height` (or `aspect-ratio`) on every image; the
  consent banner and sticky "Book a demo" reserve their space and never push
  content.
- **Measurement:** Search Console CWV report (field) + Lighthouse CI (lab) in the
  build pipeline, per language; a failing CWV blocks release (Constitution §21
  pass-rate metric).

## 12. Image optimization

- **Formats:** AVIF first, WebP fallback, with a raster fallback only where
  needed; SVG for diagrams/logos/icons (aligns with Constitution §19 vector
  imagery).
- **Responsive:** `srcset` + `sizes` for every content image; serve the right
  resolution per breakpoint; `max-width:100%` so nothing overflows.
- **Lazy-load** below-the-fold images (`loading="lazy"`); **never** lazy-load the
  LCP/hero image.
- **Dimensions:** always set `width`/`height` or `aspect-ratio` to prevent CLS.
- **Compression:** target < 150 KB for hero imagery, < 60 KB for inline
  illustrations; strip EXIF/metadata.
- **Alt text:** descriptive, keyword-aware but honest, localized per language
  (TR alt text on TR pages). Decorative images use empty `alt=""`.
- **Filenames:** descriptive, hyphenated, keyword-bearing
  (`on-prem-architecture-no-egress.avif`).
- **OG/Twitter images:** dedicated 1200×630 assets (§6–7), not reused hero crops.
- **No external copyrighted assets** — own or license everything (Constitution §19).

## 13. Structured data (governance)

The specific types and snippets are in §5. This section sets the operating rules.

- **JSON-LD only** (not Microdata/RDFa); one script block per type, injected via a
  shared template so the same page never emits conflicting duplicates.
- **Truth constraint:** structured data may only assert canonical facts. No
  fabricated `AggregateRating`, `review`, `price`, certification, or customer.
  Illustrative content (e.g. NovaMak) is never emitted as real Organization/Review
  data.
- **Localization:** `inLanguage` set correctly per page; FAQ/Article content in the
  page's language; product names stay English in both.
- **Coverage targets:** Organization + WebSite (Home), SoftwareApplication/Product
  (product & solution pages), BreadcrumbList (all except Home/Demo), FAQPage
  (Home + any Q&A page), Article (all blog).
- **QA gate:** every template validated (Rich Results Test + Schema validator)
  before ship; ongoing monitoring via Search Console's enhancements reports; target
  100% valid-structured-data pass rate (Constitution §21).

## 14. TR/EN SEO & hreflang

Turkish is a **first-class parallel track**, not a translation layer. Same
structure, same clusters, same claims, native phrasing.

**hreflang implementation:**
- Every page emits a complete return-tying `hreflang` set — EN, TR, and
  `x-default` — with **self-reference included**, on *both* language versions.
- `hreflang` values: `en` and `tr` (language only; add region only if we later
  split `tr-TR` from another Turkish market — not needed now).
- `x-default` points to the EN page (our primary) for unmatched locales.
- Delivered in the HTML `<head>` **and** in the XML sitemap (§9) — belt and braces.

**Example (`<head>` of the EN Local AI page — mirrored on the TR page):**

```html
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="en" href="https://ados.example/en/solutions/local-ai">
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="tr" href="https://ados.example/tr/solutions/local-ai">
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="x-default" href="https://ados.example/en/solutions/local-ai">
<link rel="canonical" href="https://ados.example/en/solutions/local-ai">
```

**Rules:**
- **Return links must match**: if EN points to TR, TR must point back to EN, or
  Google ignores the annotation.
- **Canonical stays in-language** (§4): EN canonical = EN URL; TR canonical = TR
  URL. `hreflang` handles the pairing.
- **No auto-redirect by IP/browser locale** that a crawler can't see; offer a
  visible TR/EN switch instead (IA §8).
- **Parity is enforced**: no page exists in one language only; a new EN page is not
  "done" until its TR pair ships (and vice versa). Bilingual parity rate is a
  health metric (Constitution §21).
- **Turkish content is authored native**, reviewed by a native Turkish reviewer
  (IA §11 / Constitution §22) — never machine-translated EN.
- **Product names stay English** in TR titles, meta, headings, and structured data.

## 15. Metadata rules

**Title tag:**
- ≤ 60 characters (aim 50–60); front-load the primary keyword; append ` | AdOS`.
- One `<h1>` per page equal to the page's primary keyword-bearing promise; the
  `<title>` may be a tighter variant.
- Unique per page and per language; TR titles are native, product names English.
- Pattern: `Primary Keyword — Qualifier | AdOS`
  - EN: `On-Premise Enterprise AI — Runs on Your Hardware | AdOS`
  - TR: `Şirket İçi Kurumsal Yapay Zeka — Kendi Donanımınızda | AdOS`

**Meta description:**
- ≤ 155 characters (aim 140–155); one per page/language; benefit + differentiator;
  include the primary keyword naturally; end with intent, not a hard sell.
- Honest voice — no banned words (revolutionary, magic, seamless, etc.).
  - EN example: "Run advertising AI 100% on your own infrastructure. AI-assisted,
    human-approved campaign drafts — offline and air-gap capable. No cloud, no
    per-token bills."
  - TR example: "Reklam yapay zekasını %100 kendi altyapınızda çalıştırın. Yapay
    zeka destekli, insan onaylı kampanya taslakları — çevrimdışı ve air-gap uyumlu."

**Other meta rules:**
- `<meta name="robots" content="index,follow">` on content; `noindex` on
  confirmation/thank-you states.
- `<html lang="en">` / `<html lang="tr">` set correctly per page.
- Canonical, hreflang, OG, Twitter tags present on every page (§4, §6, §7, §14).
- Heading hierarchy: one `h1`, ordered `h2`/`h3`, never skip levels (IA §8).
- Every page ends with exactly one primary CTA (Constitution §18/§20) — SEO copy
  never buries the conversion.

## 16. URL strategy

Inherits IA §10; restated as SEO contracts.

- **Locale prefix on every URL:** `/en/…` and `/tr/…`. The root `/` resolves to a
  language selector or the negotiated default but every indexable content URL is
  locale-prefixed.
- **Clean paths:** lowercase, hyphen-separated, no trailing slash, no file
  extensions, no IDs/dates/session tokens in content URLs.
- **Localized, keyword-bearing slugs where it helps** — but path *structure*
  mirrors the sitemap identically across languages for maintainability:
  - EN: `/en/solutions/local-ai` · `/en/solutions/on-prem-offline` · `/en/security`
  - TR: `/tr/solutions/local-ai` · `/tr/solutions/on-prem-offline` · `/tr/security`
  - Blog slugs are fully localized: `/en/blog/what-is-on-prem-ai` ↔
    `/tr/blog/sirket-ici-yapay-zeka-nedir`.
- **Turkish slugs are ASCII-folded**: `ş→s, ğ→g, ç→c, ö→o, ü→u, ı→i, İ→i` — no
  Turkish diacritics or `%`-encoding in URLs (`sirket-ici-yapay-zeka-nedir`, not
  `şirket-içi…`).
- **Hierarchy in the path** mirrors breadcrumbs and the mega-menu (max depth 3).
- **Stability:** URLs are contracts. Any rename/removal 301s to the closest
  equivalent; maintain a redirect map; never silently 404 a previously-indexed URL.
- **No parameters for content**; `?track=`/UTM allowed for routing/analytics only
  and excluded from canonical + sitemap + crawl (§4, §8).

## 17. Localization

Localization is editorial, not mechanical — the TR track earns its own rankings.

- **Native authoring:** TR copy is written by a Turkish copywriter, idiomatic and
  search-intent-matched, then reviewed by a native Turkish reviewer (IA §11).
  EN copy reviewed by a native English reviewer. Parity of claims/numbers verified
  before launch (Constitution §22).
- **Keyword research is done per language** (§1) against real Turkish search
  behavior — not translated from the EN keyword list.
- **Locale-correct technical signals:** `lang` attribute, `hreflang`, `og:locale`,
  date/number formats, TR glyph subset in fonts (§10), TR alt text (§12).
- **Terminology lock:** product names (AdOS, Company Brain) stay English in both
  languages; canonical terms (data sovereignty / veri egemenliği, on-premise /
  şirket içi) used consistently.
- **Same claims both ways:** no claim, stat, or CTA exists in only one language
  (Constitution §1/§18). CTAs use the canonical bilingual labels (Constitution §20:
  "Request a Demo" / "Demo Talep Edin", etc.).
- **Turkey-first, sovereignty-universal:** TR targets KVKK/data-residency framing
  natively; EN carries the same via GDPR/data-residency language.

## 18. Competitor keyword gap (by archetype)

We frame competitors as **archetypes**, never naming specific vendors as fact
(Constitution §13 — comparison by category, never name-and-shame). The gap
analysis finds terms an archetype ranks for where AdOS's sovereignty answer is
stronger and currently under-served.

### Archetype A — Cloud-AI assistants / hosted LLM platforms
- **They own:** "enterprise AI assistant", "AI copilot", "generative AI platform",
  broad "enterprise AI" head terms.
- **Their gap (our opening):** intents expressing *data-egress anxiety* —
  "AI without sending data to cloud", "keep data on-premise AI", "AI data
  residency", "no per-token AI", "air-gapped AI". They cannot honestly rank for
  "runs offline / on your hardware / no external API."
- **Our play:** pillar Clusters A, B, C target exactly these gap intents; comparison
  content "cloud AI vs on-prem AI: the data trade-off" (category, not vendor).

### Archetype B — On-prem point tools (single-purpose self-hosted AI/RAG)
- **They own:** narrow "self-hosted RAG", "local chatbot", "on-prem vector search"
  terms.
- **Their gap:** the *operating-system* framing — "Enterprise AI Operating System
  for Advertising", "Company Brain" (marketing-performance memory), an AI-assisted
  human-approved campaign pipeline, application-level multi-tenant. They rank for a
  feature; we rank for the platform.
- **Our play:** Clusters D, E and the "operating system, not a point tool"
  comparison; vertical solution pages that a single tool cannot cover.

### Archetype C — DIY / build-your-own (open-source stacks, internal builds)
- **They own:** developer-tutorial long-tail — "run Llama locally", "Ollama
  enterprise setup", "self-host an LLM".
- **Their gap:** the *enterprise-readiness* intents — brand governance, human
  approval gates, activity logs and approval timelines, application-level
  multi-tenant isolation, backup/DR, support, TCO of building vs buying. High-intent
  buyers searching "on-prem LLM approvals and logs" or "self-hosted AI TCO" are
  under-served.
- **Our play:** Cluster B spokes that start at the DIY query and lead to the
  build-vs-buy comparison; honest "what a DIY local LLM still lacks" content (brand
  governance, marketing-performance memory, a human-approved pipeline) — factual,
  never disparaging.

**Cross-archetype priority:** the richest gap is **cloud-AI's data-egress anxiety
intersecting DIY's enterprise-readiness gap** — buyers who want cloud capability
without cloud risk and don't want to build it themselves. That intersection is
AdOS's core keyword territory and gets first-quarter priority.

## 20. Governance & guardrails

- **CANON is binding.** No keyword target, meta description, or schema value may
  imply cloud, external API, per-token billing, millisecond frontier latency, or
  any capability AdOS does not have. When in doubt, under-claim.

  sessions, CWV pass rate, % pages with valid structured data, bilingual parity
  rate. North-star: qualified demo requests from organic.
- **Isolation:** all SEO artifacts and site changes live under `marketing/` and
  `website/`; they reference but never modify the AdOS application, its packages,
